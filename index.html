Bad USB Devices (Removable Media Threats)

A “Bad USB” attack involves an unauthorized or infected USB device that can spread malware, launch scripts, or perform social engineering.

  • Malware can spread through USB storage.
  • Users may unintentionally open unknown files from removable drives.
  • Attackers can leave malicious USB sticks in public places to lure victims.

How to Stay Safe

  • 🚫 Avoid plugging in unknown USB devices.
  • ⏹️ Disable autorun features.
  • 🔍 Scan USB drives with up‑to‑date antivirus before opening.

Phishing Emails

Phishing is a technique where attackers impersonate a trusted entity to trick you into revealing credentials or clicking malicious links.

Common Signs

  • Fake sender address (e.g., support@paypa1.com).
  • Urgent or threatening language urging immediate action.
  • Suspicious links or unexpected attachments.

Safe vs Suspicious Email Patterns

Safe:

From: john.doe@company.com
Subject: Meeting agenda for tomorrow

Suspicious:

From: security@paypa1.com
Subject: Your account is locked – click here

How to Stay Safe

  • ✅ Verify the sender’s email address.
  • 🚫 Avoid clicking unknown links or opening unexpected attachments.
  • 🔐 Enable multi‑factor authentication on all accounts.

Real‑World Awareness

Attackers often combine phishing and physical devices. For example, a malicious USB left in a lobby may contain a phishing‑laced document that, when opened, directs the user to a fake login page.

Understanding how threats intersect helps you recognize suspicious situations more quickly.
